04 / TECHNICAL WRITEUP
LAB NOTES / 001

From Reconnaissance to
Vulnerability Validation

A practical security assessment workflow: understanding the target, identifying weaknesses, validating findings and documenting the result.

01 — Reconnaissance

A security assessment starts with understanding the environment being tested. Reconnaissance helps establish what hosts, services and application entry points are exposed. Tools such as Nmap can support service discovery and provide the information needed to plan the next stage of testing.

02 — Finding weaknesses

Once the attack surface is understood, the next step is to identify potential vulnerabilities. Vulnerability scanners such as Nessus and OpenVAS can help identify known weaknesses, while manual testing is important for understanding how an issue behaves in context.

03 — Validation

A scanner finding is not automatically a confirmed vulnerability. Controlled validation helps determine whether a reported issue is actually exploitable and what its practical impact could be. In lab environments, frameworks such as Metasploit and tools such as Burp Suite can support this stage.

04 — Documentation

The final output should explain what was found, why it matters and what can be done about it. Clear documentation turns technical testing into something a security team can act on.

KEY TAKEAWAYGood security testing isn't just about finding vulnerabilities. It's about understanding evidence, validating findings and communicating risk clearly.
